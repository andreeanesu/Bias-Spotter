## B001: Confirmation Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to seek, interpret, favour, and remember information that supports what we already believe, while giving less weight to information that challenges it.
- Confused With: Belief Perseverance, Choice-Supportive Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation_bias

## B002: Anchoring Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to rely too heavily on an initial piece of information (an “anchor”) when making estimates or decisions, even when that starting point is arbitrary.
- Confused With: Framing Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_(cognitive_bias)

## B003: Availability Heuristic (Tier 1)
- Definition: A mental shortcut where we judge how common or likely something is by how easily examples come to mind, which can make vivid or recent events feel more frequent than they are.
- Confused With: Recency Bias, Representativeness Heuristic
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Availability_heuristic

## B004: Fundamental Attribution Error (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to explain other people’s actions mainly by their character or personality, while underestimating the role of the situation they are in.
- Confused With: Actor-Observer Bias, Hostile Attribution Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_attribution_error

## B005: Self-serving Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to credit our successes to our own ability or effort, and to blame our failures on outside circumstances, bad luck, or other people.
- Confused With: Actor-Observer Bias, Egocentric Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-serving_bias

## B006: Sunk Cost Fallacy (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to continue with a plan or investment because of what we have already put in (time, money, or effort), rather than because future benefits still justify it.
- Confused With: Loss Aversion, Status Quo Bias, Endowment Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunk_cost#Loss_aversion_and_the_sunk_cost_fallacy

## B007: Negativity Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency for negative events, information, or emotions to affect us more strongly than positive ones of equal intensity.
- Confused With: Loss Aversion
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negativity_bias

## B008: Halo Effect (Tier 1)
- Definition: When a positive overall impression of someone (or something) spills over and makes us rate their other traits more favourably than the evidence supports.
- Confused With: Horn Effect, Authority Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_effect

## B009: Horn Effect (Tier 1)
- Definition: When a negative overall impression of someone (or something) spills over and makes us rate their other traits more harshly than the evidence supports.
- Confused With: Halo Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_effect

## B010: Survivorship Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to draw conclusions from the people or cases that “made it,” while overlooking those that failed, dropped out, or were never visible, which can distort what we think works.
- Confused With: Availability Heuristic
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivorship_bias

## B011: Hindsight Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency, after an outcome is known, to see it as more predictable than it really was, and to believe we “knew it all along.”
- Confused With: Overconfidence Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindsight_bias

## B012: False Consensus Effect (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to overestimate how widely others share our beliefs, preferences, and behaviours.
- Confused With: In-group Bias, Naive Realism
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_consensus_effect

## B013: In-group Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to favour people who belong to our own group, giving them more trust, warmth, or benefit of the doubt than we give outsiders.
- Confused With: Out-group Homogeneity Bias, False Consensus Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-group_favoritism

## B014: Out-group Homogeneity Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to see people in other groups as more alike than they are, while seeing members of our own group as more varied and individual.
- Confused With: In-group Bias, Representativeness Heuristic
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-group_homogeneity

## B015: Framing Effect (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency for the same facts to lead to different choices depending on how they are worded or presented, for example as gains versus losses.
- Confused With: Anchoring Bias, Loss Aversion
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framing_effect_(psychology)

## B016: Loss Aversion (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency for losses to feel more painful than gains of the same size feel rewarding, which can make us overly cautious about potential losses.
- Confused With: Sunk Cost Fallacy, Endowment Effect, Negativity Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_aversion

## B017: Optimism Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to overestimate how likely good things are to happen to us, and to underestimate our personal risk of bad outcomes compared with other people.
- Confused With: Overconfidence Effect, Planning Fallacy, Illusion of Control
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimism_bias

## B018: Status Quo Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to prefer the current state of affairs and to treat “keeping things as they are” as the default choice, even when change might be better.
- Confused With: Sunk Cost Fallacy, Endowment Effect, Normalcy Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo_bias

## B019: Dunning-Kruger Effect (Tier 1)
- Definition: A pattern where people with limited skill or knowledge in an area overestimate how competent they are, partly because the same gaps that cause mistakes also hide those mistakes from view.
- Confused With: Overconfidence Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunning%E2%80%93Kruger_effect

## B020: Authority Bias (Tier 1)
- Definition: The tendency to trust or follow the views of someone seen as an authority more than the strength of their arguments alone would justify.
- Confused With: Halo Effect, Bandwagon Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority_bias

## B021: Planning Fallacy (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to underestimate how long a task will take and how many obstacles will appear, even when similar tasks have overrun before.
- Confused With: Optimism Bias, Overconfidence Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_fallacy

## B022: Recency Bias (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to give recent information more weight than older information, even when the longer track record is more informative.
- Confused With: Availability Heuristic
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recency_bias

## B023: Bandwagon Effect (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to adopt a belief or behaviour because many other people are doing so, treating popularity as a signal that it must be right.
- Confused With: Authority Bias, In-group Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandwagon_effect

## B024: Mere Exposure Effect (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to like people, ideas, or things more simply because we have encountered them before, even without stronger reasons to prefer them.
- Confused With: Status Quo Bias, Illusory Truth Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mere-exposure_effect

## B025: Endowment Effect (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to value something more highly once we own it than we would if the same item belonged to someone else.
- Confused With: Loss Aversion, Status Quo Bias, Sunk Cost Fallacy
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endowment_effect

## B026: Gambler's Fallacy (Tier 2)
- Definition: The belief that a random event is “due” after a streak of opposite outcomes, as if chance keeps a short-run balance sheet.
- Confused With: Clustering Illusion, Illusion of Control
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambler%27s_fallacy

## B027: Clustering Illusion (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to see meaningful patterns or streaks in small samples of random or unrelated events.
- Confused With: Gambler's Fallacy, Illusory Correlation
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clustering_illusion

## B028: Representativeness Heuristic (Tier 2)
- Definition: A mental shortcut where we judge probability by how much something resembles a familiar stereotype or story, instead of using actual base rates.
- Confused With: Base Rate Neglect, Availability Heuristic
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representativeness_heuristic

## B029: Base Rate Neglect (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to ignore general statistical information about how common something is, and to focus instead on specific, vivid details.
- Confused With: Representativeness Heuristic, Availability Heuristic
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_rate_fallacy

## B030: Peak-End Rule (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to judge an experience mainly by its most intense moment and how it ended, rather than by the whole duration.
- Confused With: Negativity Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak%E2%80%93end_rule

## B031: Choice-Supportive Bias (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency, after we have chosen an option, to remember it more favourably and to downplay its downsides or the appeal of alternatives.
- Confused With: Confirmation Bias, Cognitive Dissonance
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choice-supportive_bias

## B032: Illusion of Control (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to overestimate how much influence our actions have over outcomes that are largely random or outside our control.
- Confused With: Optimism Bias, Gambler's Fallacy
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusion_of_control

## B033: Spotlight Effect (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to overestimate how much other people notice our appearance, mistakes, or behaviour.
- Confused With: Illusion of Transparency, Egocentric Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotlight_effect

## B034: Cognitive Dissonance (Tier 2)
- Definition: The mental discomfort that arises when our actions conflict with our beliefs, often leading us to justify, minimise, or reframe what we did.
- Confused With: Choice-Supportive Bias, Belief Perseverance
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_dissonance

## B035: Belief Perseverance (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to hold on to a belief even after the evidence that supported it has been weakened or removed.
- Confused With: Confirmation Bias, Semmelweis Reflex
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief_perseverance

## B036: Normalcy Bias (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to underestimate the likelihood of serious disruption and to assume that life will continue in the usual way.
- Confused With: Status Quo Bias, Optimism Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalcy_bias

## B037: Curse of Knowledge (Tier 2)
- Definition: The difficulty of remembering what it was like not to know something, which can make experts skip steps that beginners still need.
- Confused With: False Consensus Effect, Egocentric Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_knowledge

## B038: Illusory Correlation (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to perceive a relationship between two things when none exists, or to see a stronger link than the evidence supports.
- Confused With: Clustering Illusion, Confirmation Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusory_correlation

## B039: Conjunction Fallacy (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency to judge a detailed, specific scenario as more probable than a broader one, even though added detail usually makes an event less likely.
- Confused With: Representativeness Heuristic
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunction_fallacy

## B040: Illusory Truth Effect (Tier 2)
- Definition: The tendency for repeated statements to feel more true, simply because they have become familiar.
- Confused With: Mere Exposure Effect, Confirmation Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusory_truth_effect

## B041: Actor-Observer Bias (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to explain other people’s behaviour by their character, while explaining our own behaviour by the situation we were in.
- Confused With: Fundamental Attribution Error, Self-serving Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor%E2%80%93observer_asymmetry

## B042: Bias Blind Spot (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to recognise cognitive biases more easily in other people than in ourselves.
- Confused With: Naive Realism, Overconfidence Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bias_blind_spot

## B043: Naive Realism (Tier 3)
- Definition: The belief that we see the world objectively, and that people who disagree with us must be uninformed, irrational, or biased.
- Confused With: False Consensus Effect, Bias Blind Spot
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na%C3%AFve_realism_(psychology)

## B044: Just-World Hypothesis (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to assume the world is fair and that people usually get what they deserve, which can lead us to blame victims or downplay luck.
- Confused With: Fundamental Attribution Error, Hindsight Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just-world_hypothesis

## B045: Hostile Attribution Bias (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to interpret ambiguous actions from others as intentionally hostile, unkind, or personally directed.
- Confused With: Fundamental Attribution Error, Negativity Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostile_attribution_bias

## B046: Egocentric Bias (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to remember our own role, effort, or importance in joint events as larger than it really was.
- Confused With: Self-serving Bias, Spotlight Effect
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egocentric_bias

## B047: Illusion of Transparency (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to overestimate how easily other people can read our emotions, thoughts, or internal state.
- Confused With: Spotlight Effect, Egocentric Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusion_of_transparency

## B048: Hyperbolic Discounting (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to prefer smaller rewards now over larger rewards later, more steeply than a calm long-term plan would suggest.
- Confused With: Optimism Bias, Restraint Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_discounting

## B049: Ambiguity Effect (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to avoid options when the probability of a good outcome is unclear, even if those options could be better than a known alternative.
- Confused With: Status Quo Bias, Loss Aversion
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambiguity_effect

## B050: Identifiable Victim Effect (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to feel and give more help in response to one identifiable person in need than to large groups described only with statistics.
- Confused With: Availability Heuristic, Negativity Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identifiable_victim_effect

## B051: Restraint Bias (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to overestimate how well we will resist temptation when the moment actually arrives.
- Confused With: Overconfidence Effect, Optimism Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restraint_bias

## B052: Reactance (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to push back when we feel our freedom is being restricted, sometimes by doing the opposite of what we are urged to do.
- Confused With: Status Quo Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactance_(psychology)

## B053: Ostrich Effect (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to avoid looking at information that might confirm a problem we would rather not face.
- Confused With: Optimism Bias, Normalcy Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrich_effect

## B054: Zero-Sum Bias (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to treat a situation as if one person’s gain must mean another person’s loss, even when both could benefit.
- Confused With: In-group Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-sum_thinking

## B055: Moral Luck (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to judge how good or bad a decision was partly by how lucky or unlucky the outcome turned out to be.
- Confused With: Hindsight Bias, Fundamental Attribution Error
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_luck

## B056: Texas Sharpshooter Fallacy (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to notice a cluster in data first, then treat that cluster as if it had been predicted, drawing the target after the shots.
- Confused With: Clustering Illusion, Illusory Correlation
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_sharpshooter_fallacy

## B057: Overconfidence Effect (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to be more confident in our judgments than our actual accuracy usually supports.
- Confused With: Dunning-Kruger Effect, Optimism Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overconfidence_effect

## B058: Pro-Innovation Bias (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to assume a new idea, tool, or innovation will help more than it will, while underestimating its limits and side effects.
- Confused With: Optimism Bias, Authority Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-innovation_bias

## B059: Semmelweis Reflex (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to reject new evidence or ideas mainly because they challenge established beliefs, habits, or authority.
- Confused With: Belief Perseverance, Confirmation Bias
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semmelweis_reflex

## B060: Risk Compensation (Tier 3)
- Definition: The tendency to take greater risks when we feel protected, which can partly cancel out the safety we gained.
- Confused With: Optimism Bias, Illusion of Control
- Further Reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_compensation
