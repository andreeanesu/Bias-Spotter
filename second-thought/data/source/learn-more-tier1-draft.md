## B001 — Confirmation Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to seek, interpret, favour, and remember information that supports what we already believe, while giving less weight to information that challenges it.
- Usually confused with: Belief Perseverance, Choice-Supportive Bias
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation_bias

## B002 — Anchoring Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to rely too heavily on an initial piece of information (an “anchor”) when making estimates or decisions, even when that starting point is arbitrary.
- Usually confused with: Framing Effect
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_(cognitive_bias)

## B003 — Availability Heuristic
- In clearer terms: A mental shortcut where we judge how common or likely something is by how easily examples come to mind, which can make vivid or recent events feel more frequent than they are.
- Usually confused with: Recency Bias, Representativeness Heuristic
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Availability_heuristic

## B004 — Fundamental Attribution Error
- In clearer terms: The tendency to explain other people’s actions mainly by their character or personality, while underestimating the role of the situation they are in.
- Usually confused with: Actor-Observer Bias, Hostile Attribution Bias
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_attribution_error

## B005 — Self-serving Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to credit our successes to our own ability or effort, and to blame our failures on outside circumstances, bad luck, or other people.
- Usually confused with: Actor-Observer Bias, Egocentric Bias
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-serving_bias

## B006 — Sunk Cost Fallacy
- In clearer terms: The tendency to continue with a plan or investment because of what we have already put in (time, money, or effort), rather than because future benefits still justify it.
- Usually confused with: Loss Aversion, Status Quo Bias, Endowment Effect
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunk_cost#Loss_aversion_and_the_sunk_cost_fallacy

## B007 — Negativity Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency for negative events, information, or emotions to affect us more strongly than positive ones of equal intensity.
- Usually confused with: Loss Aversion
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negativity_bias

## B008 — Halo Effect
- In clearer terms: When a positive overall impression of someone (or something) spills over and makes us rate their other traits more favourably than the evidence supports.
- Usually confused with: Horn Effect, Authority Bias
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_effect

## B009 — Horn Effect
- In clearer terms: When a negative overall impression of someone (or something) spills over and makes us rate their other traits more harshly than the evidence supports.
- Usually confused with: Halo Effect
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_effect

## B010 — Survivorship Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to draw conclusions from the people or cases that “made it,” while overlooking those that failed, dropped out, or were never visible — which can distort what we think works.
- Usually confused with: Availability Heuristic
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivorship_bias

## B011 — Hindsight Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency, after an outcome is known, to see it as more predictable than it really was, and to believe we “knew it all along.”
- Usually confused with: Overconfidence Effect
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindsight_bias

## B012 — False Consensus Effect
- In clearer terms: The tendency to overestimate how widely others share our beliefs, preferences, and behaviours.
- Usually confused with: In-group Bias, Naive Realism
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_consensus_effect

## B013 — In-group Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to favour people who belong to our own group — giving them more trust, warmth, or benefit of the doubt than we give outsiders.
- Usually confused with: Out-group Homogeneity Bias, False Consensus Effect
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-group_favoritism

## B014 — Out-group Homogeneity Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to see people in other groups as more alike than they are, while seeing members of our own group as more varied and individual.
- Usually confused with: In-group Bias, Representativeness Heuristic
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-group_homogeneity

## B015 — Framing Effect
- In clearer terms: The tendency for the same facts to lead to different choices depending on how they are worded or presented — for example as gains versus losses.
- Usually confused with: Anchoring Bias, Loss Aversion
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framing_effect_(psychology)

## B016 — Loss Aversion
- In clearer terms: The tendency for losses to feel more painful than gains of the same size feel rewarding, which can make us overly cautious about potential losses.
- Usually confused with: Sunk Cost Fallacy, Endowment Effect, Negativity Bias
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_aversion

## B017 — Optimism Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to overestimate how likely good things are to happen to us, and to underestimate our personal risk of bad outcomes compared with other people.
- Usually confused with: Overconfidence Effect, Planning Fallacy, Illusion of Control
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimism_bias

## B018 — Status Quo Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to prefer the current state of affairs and to treat “keeping things as they are” as the default choice, even when change might be better.
- Usually confused with: Sunk Cost Fallacy, Endowment Effect, Normalcy Bias
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo_bias

## B019 — Dunning-Kruger Effect
- In clearer terms: A pattern where people with limited skill or knowledge in an area overestimate how competent they are, partly because the same gaps that cause mistakes also hide those mistakes from view.
- Usually confused with: Overconfidence Effect
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunning%E2%80%93Kruger_effect

## B020 — Authority Bias
- In clearer terms: The tendency to trust or follow the views of someone seen as an authority more than the strength of their arguments alone would justify.
- Usually confused with: Halo Effect, Bandwagon Effect
- Further reading: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority_bias
